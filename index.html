One sacred verse a day, with its meaning and the story behind it.

  • One verse a day from the Bhagavad Gita, the Ramayana, selected Mahabharata passages, stotrams, Rig Veda hymns and mantras, in Sanskrit with a clear translation

Collect the vahanas

Every devata has a vehicle, and each one joins you as your streak grows: Mushika the mouse at one day, Nandi the bull at seven, Simha the lion at fourteen, and more, up to a year. There are 19 in all, and collecting them is free for everyone.

Your Ishta Devata comes in three forms: Little, Classic and Fierce. Choosing one is free, always.

Widgets on your Home Screen

Smriti has widgets: small cards you place on your Home Screen. The small one shows your streak and a vahana, and tells you when today's verse is waiting. Another shows today's verse. With Smriti Plus there are more, with your Ishta Devata, a Darshan card, and a bigger one with your week. On iPhone there are Lock Screen widgets too.

  • iPhone and iPad: touch and hold an empty spot on the Home Screen, tap +, search for Smriti.
  • Android: touch and hold an empty spot on the home screen, tap Widgets, find Smriti.

No account. No tracking. No ads today (we cannot promise that for ever: see the Terms of Use). Smriti has no server: your streak, favourites and notes stay on your device.

Frequently asked questions

Which devices does Smriti run on?

iPhone and iPad (iOS 26 or later) and Android phones and tablets. Both are being released; the app is not in the stores yet.

Is Smriti free?

Yes. Today's verse, your streak and calendar, one daily reminder, the small widgets, the whole Karma Yoga part of the Gita (chapters 1 to 6), a taste of every other work, all 19 vahanas and your Ishta Devata are free, with no ads today. Smriti Plus opens the whole library, every widget, more themes and sync.

See everything Free and Plus include →

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and no login.

Who wrote the translations?

They are written by AI for Smriti, in our own words, and labelled as AI-generated in the app. They are not yet reviewed by a Sanskrit scholar. The Sanskrit text comes from public-domain editions on Wikisource. If you find a mistake, use "Suggest a better translation" in the app and we will look at it.

Where is my data stored?

On your device. On iPhone, Smriti Plus can also keep it in your own private iCloud. Nothing goes to a server of ours, because we have none. See the Privacy Policy.
